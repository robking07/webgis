# Dataset scout: immigration enforcement and non-governmental sources (2026-10-01)

Research only. Nothing here has been ingested. All sources were visited on 2026-10-01 unless a row says otherwise.

**Context that shapes the recommendations**

- webgis is deployed **publicly** (SPEC §1, §11). Any license that limits use to "personal", "non-dissemination" or "no database" rules out the dataset. So does "non-commercial" until Robbie decides how he reads NC for a free public site.
- SPEC §2 says Phase 1 uses **free federal data only**. Everything non-federal below is a Phase 2+ candidate, or needs Robbie to widen Phase 1.
- Per SPEC §10, an unclear license is a stop-and-log item. Each one is flagged ⚠.
- Supabase free tier is 500 MB, so only aggregated tables are realistic. Point layers go into PMTiles on R2.
- Weird Insights: **no ICE or police-violence data should be fed into the humorous pairing engine.** Exclude it from the insight generator by registry flag.

Value score: 1–5. It weighs usefulness to Robbie, how cleanly it joins to Census geography, license clarity and maintenance.

## Part 1: Immigration enforcement

### 1. ICE detention facilities: Deportation Data Project (DDP) · score 5

- **URL:** https://deportationdata.org/docs/ice/codebook-facilities.html · repo https://github.com/deportationdata/ice-detention-facilities (pushed 2026-09-30)
- **Maintainers:** Graeme Blair and Hayley Labia (UCLA), with a team of undergraduate coders. DDP is a UC Berkeley Law / UCLA project.
- **What it is:** every facility that held at least one civil immigration detainee since 2025-01-01, plus facilities newly listed on ICE's site. That includes dedicated centers, IGSA jails, private CDFs, BOP/USMS, hold rooms, hospitals and hotels. Each row is one ICE detention facility code, so it joins to the detention microdata.
- **Method:** collated from ICE web snapshots (2026-02 through 2026-08), many FOIA lists, Marshall Project, Vera, HIFLD prisons/jails, CMS hospitals and BJS Census of Jails. Geocoded with ArcGIS and Google, then **every facility manually reviewed by multiple coders**. County assigned from 2024 TIGER, plus federal judicial district and circuit.
- **Format:** `data/facilities-latest.parquet`, `facilities-latest-sf.parquet`, `facilities-latest-shp.zip`. All are **Git LFS** files, so fetch with `git lfs` or the media URL; the raw URL returns a pointer.
- **License:** site footer reads "Licensed CC-0 (no rights reserved)". ⚠ This repo has no LICENSE file (its sibling repos `ice`, `ice-offices` and `ice-287g` are CC0-1.0). The build also pulls `facilities-vera.parquet` as an input, and Vera's data is non-dissemination licensed (see Do not ingest). Ask DDP to confirm that the published facility table is CC0.
- **Privacy:** institutions only, no people. Safe.
- **Fit:** point layer filtered by facility type (dedicated, IGSA, federal, hold, medical, hotel) and by AOR. Size the points by ADP (#9 or DDP stints). Compare with ACS foreign-born or non-citizen share by county, and with 287(g) coverage.

### 2. ICE field offices and areas of responsibility: DDP · score 5

- **URL:** https://deportationdata.org/docs/ice/codebook-offices.html · repo `deportationdata/ice-offices` (CC0-1.0, pushed 2026-06-17)
- **What:** ERO field offices and check-in sub-offices as points (from the ICE site, Sept 2025), plus AOR polygons in two forms: one polygon per field office (`ice-aor-shp`), and **a county-level file where each row is a TIGER 2024 county with GEOID mapped to its field office** (`ice-aor-county-shp`, about 11.6 MB). The AOR map was originally from UW Center for Human Rights. Notes record the caveats (Houston taken as 57 counties; the Saipan/CNMI ambiguity).
- **Fit:** the county→AOR crosswalk is the key piece. It lets AOR-level statistics (arrests, ATD) be drawn on webgis's existing county geometry, dissolved to 25 AORs. Field-office points are public government offices. Check-in offices are also public, but showing them invites "where to wait for people" use, so default them off.

### 3. ICE 287(g) agreements: DDP · score 5

- **URL:** https://github.com/deportationdata/ice-287g (CC0-1.0, pushed 2026-10-01, actively maintained)
- **What:** every archived version of ICE's 287(g) participating-agencies sheet, from 2002 to today. One row per signed MOA with model (Jail Enforcement, Task Force, Warrant Service), signed and removed dates, status, and **Census `geoid`/`geoid_type` (state, county, place)** plus geometry. There is also an agency-level history table. Formats: GeoParquet, shp, xlsx, dta, sav.
- **Privacy:** government agencies only. Safe.
- **Fit:** a county/place choropleth of "has an active 287(g) agreement, by model", plus a time slider. It is a strong explanatory layer next to ICE arrests by AOR, which are much higher in 287(g)-heavy states.

### 4. ICE enforcement microdata (arrests, detainers, detentions, encounters, removals): DDP · score 5, aggregate only

- **URL:** https://deportationdata.org/data/ice.html · processed: https://deportationdata.org/data/processed/ice.html · codebook https://deportationdata.org/docs/ice/codebook.html
- **Provenance:** ICE records released under FOIA litigation (*Hausman v. ICE*). The latest release runs **through early March 2026** (the processed page says through 2026-03-06). A search result mentioned a release through 2026-08-06, published 2026-08-21, but the site pages I read don't show it yet, so I couldn't confirm it. There is also a 2012–2023 linked ACLU set (2.6 GB zip). Releases come irregularly, whenever litigation produces data.
- **Geography:** `apprehension_state` (with DDP's `apprehension_state_filled_in`), AOR, detention facility code, and a new **free-text arrest city** field. DDP warns it is "manually entered… same city is often entered in different ways", about 4% missing since 2025. No coordinates, no county.
- **Quality caveats from DDP:** removals data reliability is questioned; `apprehension_type` was not released this time.
- **License:** "Licensed CC-0 (no rights reserved)". Suggested citation: "government data provided by ICE in response to a FOIA request to the Deportation Data Project…"
- **Privacy:** the records are anonymized but individual-level (birth year, citizenship, gender, dates, facility). **Do not ingest the microdata into webgis.** Build aggregates offline in the ingest script:
  - arrests and detainers by **state × month** and **AOR × month** (with criminal-history breakdown where present);
  - detention **book-ins and ADP by facility × month**, using DDP's stints table, as recommended for facility-level work;
  - suppress cells under 10 (about the small-cell rule) in sparse AORs and months.
  - A county-level arrest layer would require normalizing the city field into places and counties. It's doable for big cities but lossy. Mark it experimental, or skip it.
- **Caution on "detentions by county":** a county choropleth of detentions measures where *facilities* are, not where people were arrested. The opxie1/icedetention pipeline (MIT) does exactly that. If it's used, label it "people held in facilities located in this county".

### 9. ICE detention statistics (official): ICE · score 4

- **URL:** https://www.ice.gov/detain/detention-management. "Detention FY 2026 YTD, Alternatives to Detention FY 2026 YTD and Facilities FY 2026 YTD, Footnotes (221 KB)" is YTD as of 2026-07-20. FY2019–FY2025 are also posted.
- **What:** official ADP by facility, with facility address, type, AOR, inspection info, and ATD by AOR. Protected .xlsx. Updated roughly every two weeks to monthly during the FY, then locked at year end. DDP republishes parsed versions in `deportationdata/ice-detention-management` (ADP by agency, ATD by AOR, bond stats…).
- **License:** US government work, public domain. It fits Phase 1's federal-only rule, so it's the most Phase-1-compatible ICE layer.
- **Fit:** gives the facility points (#1) their ADP. Federal publishing has been erratic since 2025, so keep the DDP mirror as a fallback.

### Observer and community-collected data

Assessed at Robbie's request. The common risk: these datasets record **where and when agents were seen, often with vehicle descriptions or plates, and sometimes where people were detained** (homes, workplaces, bus stops). Re-publishing them on a polished public map would:
(a) expose private addresses and the people detained there, many of whom have pending cases;
(b) provide real-time or near-real-time location of identifiable officers, which drew the "doxxing" campaigns that took down People over Papers;
(c) put Robbie in a legally contested area (DHS has publicly threatened prosecution of ICE-tracking apps; I didn't verify the legal merits). It also creates a stable scrape target that outlasts the organizers' own moderation.

**Responsible aggregate path for observer data:** the safe product is *counts of verified incidents by county (or tract) × month, lagged ≥30 days, cells < 5 suppressed, with no agent, vehicle or address detail.* I found **no public dataset that already publishes it in that form.** The Tucson project is the closest candidate to ask. For national coverage, DDP's FOIA-based arrests by AOR/state × month is a better-founded alternative. It counts what ICE recorded rather than what observers saw, and its geography is coarse.

## Part 2: Broader sweep

### 5. EJScreen v2.3 archive (re: D-04) · score 4

- **Mirrors found:**
  - **Harvard Dataverse, HELD collection**: doi:10.7910/DVN/RLR5AX, published 2025-02-28 (v4). Author "EPA". **License: CC0 1.0.** Notes: "EJScreen data was downloaded by Dylan Carlson Sirvent León on December 3rd, 2024", plus a clone of the USEPA/EJScreen GitHub. 3,249 files, about 7.5 GB (zipped folders available).
  - **CHORDS (NIEHS-linked)**: doi:10.7910/DVN/LWVEZU (2026-01-09), CC0, metadata/pointer only (2 files).
  - **PEDP** (screening-tools.com → publicenvirodata.org): "an initial reconstruction of Version 2.3 of EPA's EJScreen. Public access to the tool from EPA was discontinued on February 5, 2025." It links to the Harvard Dataverse as the underlying data.
- **Assessment for D-04:** provenance is now traceable: the EPA FTP snapshot of 2024-12-03 → HELD Dataverse, CC0. EJScreen was a US federal work (17 U.S.C. §105), so there was no copyright to begin with, and CC0 on the mirror is consistent. The remaining risk is **integrity, not licensing**: a third-party mirror, with no EPA checksum. Mitigations: label the layer "EJScreen 2.3 (EPA, archived Dec 2024, via Harvard Dataverse)", record the DOI and file hashes in the registry, and spot-check a few block groups against a Wayback capture of the EPA site. **Recommend Robbie close D-04 as "OK to ingest, archived-and-frozen".** It's still his call. Note that CEJST (already on webgis) and EJScreen overlap; EJScreen adds block-group pollution indicators that CEJST lacks.

### 6. County Health Rankings & Roadmaps · score 4

- **URL:** https://zenodo.org/records/17832488 (published 2025-12-05); site https://www.countyhealthrankings.org
- **What:** about 90 county-level health, behavior, clinical-care, socioeconomic and physical-environment measures, 2010–2025 (per-year zips, 5–53 MB).
- **License:** the Zenodo deposit is **CC BY 4.0**. The website's own terms are *non-commercial*: "personal or non-profit purposes" and "may not be copied or otherwise used for commercial use (including… to train AI models…)". **Use the Zenodo copy** to stay clean.
- **Note:** CHR&R program funding **ends December 2026**, so this is effectively a final archive. Download now.
- **Fit:** county choropleths. It overlaps with CDC PLACES (which is tract-level), but adds premature death, uninsured, PCP ratio, broadband, housing and income inequality. Many measures are themselves federal re-cuts; prefer the original source where webgis has it.

### 7. Opportunity Atlas · score 4

- **URL:** https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/ces/data/public-use-data/opportunity-atlas-data-tables.html (Census CES with Opportunity Insights)
- **What:** Module 1, tract-level adult outcomes (income rank, incarceration, teen birth…) by parental income × race × sex for 1978–83 cohorts. Module 2 (2024), county mobility trends for 1978–1992 cohorts. Credit outcomes added 2025. CSV/Stata.
- **License:** Census Bureau public-use data, so federal and Phase-1 compatible. Static, with periodic module additions.
- **Fit:** tract choropleth, e.g. "kid from a low-income family: expected income rank". It pairs well with commute time (ACS/LODES). The Chetty paper famously links commute time and mobility, which makes a credible insight.

### 8. Eviction Lab · score 4

- **URL:** https://data-downloads.evictionlab.org/ (S3 bucket `eviction-lab-data-downloads`); ETS https://evictionlab.org/eviction-tracking/get-the-data/
- **What:** (a) modeled **eviction filing and judgment estimates for every county 2000–2018**, plus state, place, tract and block-group files (`estimating-eviction-prevalance-across-us/`, `data-for-analysis/`, including tracts re-based to 2020). (b) The **Eviction Tracking System**: weekly and monthly filings by tract/ZIP for about 40 cities and states, including Minneapolis–Saint Paul and Minnesota, most updated 09/2026.
- **License:** bucket page: "This data is shared under the terms of the Open Data Commons Attribution License (ODC-BY 1.0)", with a required citation (Gromis et al.). ⚠ The ETS page is email-gated ("Enter your email to access the data"), and the main site says "© 2018 Eviction Lab. All rights reserved." Confirm that ETS CSVs fall under the same ODC-BY. The S3 `ets/` folder only holds 2020–2021.
- **Privacy:** aggregated counts. "Sealed" rows for suppressed cases. Safe.
- **Fit:** county choropleth of 2018 filing rate (national); tract/month detail for ETS sites. Natural pairing with ACS rent burden.

### 10. Overture Maps Places (OSM-derived amenity proxy) · score 4

- **URL:** https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/ (release v2.0.0)
- **License:** Places from Meta, Microsoft and others: "Available under CDLA Permissive 2.0". Some sources carry their own terms (e.g. Foursquare "All rights reserved", so check which sources survive in the US extract). Divisions, buildings and transportation themes are ODbL ("© OpenStreetMap contributors").
- **Fit:** derive counts per tract of groceries, pharmacies, schools, parks and transit stops as a **walkability/amenity proxy**. Ship tract aggregates, not tens of millions of points. The EPA National Walkability Index is still online (https://www.epa.gov/smartgrowth/smart-location-mapping, block group) and is the federal-first alternative for Phase 1.

### 11. Transit service: Mobility Database / Transitland · score 4

- **Mobility Database** (MobilityData): catalog of more than 6,000 GTFS/GTFS-RT/GBFS feeds. Catalog repo `MobilityData/mobility-database-catalogs` is **Apache-2.0**. API needs a free account. Each feed keeps **its own agency license**, so you have to check per feed.
- **Transitland** (Interline): terms put the burden on the consumer ("it is the responsibility of each Transitland data and API consumer to confirm any license requirements imposed by the source feeds"). API key required.
- **Fit:** build tract-level "weekday transit trips per hour within ½ mile" from GTFS in an ingest script. Highly relevant to Robbie's day job. It needs a per-feed license audit, so start with one metro. Use Mobility Database as the source list, not Transitland.

### 12. USDA Food Access (SRAM) · score 4

- **URL:** https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-access-research-atlas/download-the-data. "2025 SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map (SRAM)… Last Updated 7/27/2026", 2020 tracts. The legacy 2019 LRAM is on 2010 tracts.
- Federal, public domain, still online. Tract choropleth of low-access flags. Phase-1 compatible.

### 13. Mapping Police Violence · score 3

- **URL:** https://mappingpoliceviolence.org (Campaign Zero). Last updated 2026-09-16. Covers 2013–present; about 1,300–1,400 killings a year. Data is an Airtable shared view, downloaded by hand ("…tap 'Download CSV'"). Fields include location; the methodology page didn't show coordinate and tract field names, so check the CSV.
- **License:** none stated. The methodology says: "We believe that the data we collect, code, and make available should be public and free to access. It is not and should not be owned by any platform." That's permissive in spirit but ⚠ not a license. Ask Campaign Zero for written OK plus the attribution form.
- **Privacy:** victims are named. Show **county or state rates only**; leave out names and point locations.
- **Fit:** county/state rate per million vs ACS race composition. Since WaPo "Fatal Force" stopped in 2024, MPV is the remaining national source.

### 14. Social Capital Atlas · score 3

- Economic connectedness, cohesiveness and civic engagement by county and ZIP (2022, Meta + Opportunity Insights), from HDX (https://data.humdata.org/dataset/social-capital-atlas). HDX license field: "Other: Please see LICENSE.PDF". I couldn't extract the PDF text in this environment. ⚠ Read it before ingesting.

### 15. Ookla Speedtest open data · score 3

- AWS Open Data `s3://ookla-open-data`, quarterly, z16 tiles (about 611 m), latest 2026-Q2. **License "CC BY-NC-SA 4.0"**, which carries non-commercial and share-alike obligations. ⚠ Decide whether webgis is "non-commercial". ShareAlike would also bind any derived tract layer. M-Lab is likewise CC BY-NC-SA for site material. The **FCC National Broadband Map** (federal) is the clean alternative for availability; Ookla adds measured speed.

### Others checked, not shortlisted

- **Gun Violence Archive**: "Data from the Gun Violence Archive is copyrighted, and our data aggregation is proprietary. Our data is not in the public domain or subject to Creative Commons licensing. Anyone is allowed to use GVA data either by utilizing the query function and adhering to the limits of that process or contacting GVA for either partnership licensing or use agreement." Bulk use needs a license agreement. **Blocked without one.**
- **National Equity Atlas** (PolicyLink/USC ERI): "© 2026 National Equity Atlas. All rights reserved." The data-download and terms URLs I tried returned 404. Most indicators are ACS/PUMS re-cuts, so **rebuild from ACS instead**.
- **Opportunity Insights non-Census files** (Social Capital, Economic Tracker): see #14. The Economic Tracker is mostly COVID-era and stale.

| **TRAC Immigration** (tracreports.org) | Terms prohibit it outright: "This license does not permit Authorized Users to download data for the purpose of creating a new database or using the data in an existing database", and users may not "scrape, crawl… download in bulk… aggregate, compile, reproduce, mirror, cache, republish, redistribute, or incorporate substantial portions of TRAC content or data into another database". Unverified, no license, legally contested. Not recommended at any point-level granularity. |
| **DDP individual-level records as-is** | CC0 and anonymized, but per-person rows; only state/AOR/facility × month aggregates with small-cell suppression should reach webgis. |
| **Gun Violence Archive** | Proprietary. Use needs a GVA agreement. |
| **Marshall Project `dhs_immigration_detention`** | No license, last updated 2022, superseded by DDP (which already cites it). |
